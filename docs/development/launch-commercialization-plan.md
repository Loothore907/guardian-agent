## Purpose

This plan prepares Agentic Guardian to convert competition attention into durable
project trust, useful relationships, design-partner conversations, and possible
revenue without distracting from or overstating the security implementation.

It runs beside the development roadmap. It does not create permission to add
features, publish a release, transfer a repository, change DNS, contact third
parties, enter contracts, or make security claims. Those actions still require
their normal evidence, review, and owner authorization.

The operating sequence is:

```text
working implementation
  -> reproducible evidence
    -> accurate public explanation
      -> visitor trust
        -> contact or trial
          -> discovery conversation
            -> bounded design-partner pilot
```

Public communication may lag verified implementation. It may never lead it.

Completing an `Agent` task does not authorize its publication. Completing a plan
item that says `Owner console` does not authorize Codex to approximate the action
through another tool or account.

## Guardrails

1. The implementation and security-claims matrix remain authoritative.
2. No launch task may delay a required security, evidence, or submission gate.
3. The words `Enforced`, `verified`, `credential-safe`, `prevents`, and equivalent
   security language must agree with the exact supported runtime and evidence.
4. The competition prototype is not marketed as a production authorization,
   credential-management, or compliance product.
5. The Apache-licensed core remains a complete, useful, inspectable project. A
   future commercial layer must add organizational or operational value rather
   than withhold a safety control required for credible use of the core.
6. Contact forms, analytics, mailing lists, and customer notes must not collect
   credentials, private repository content, agent transcripts, or unnecessary
   personal data.
7. Prospective customer information stays out of the public repository.
8. Prize placement, stars, and traffic are attention signals, not evidence of
   product-market fit or security.
9. Repository ownership, DNS, GitHub App scope, demo targets, and release state
   are never changed as an incidental branding step.
10. External publication and account mutations are batched into explicit owner
    console journeys with a rollback or recovery note where practical.

## Outcomes and measurements

### Submission-ready outcome

By the October 28 internal lock, a first-time visitor must be able to:

1. understand the problem and differentiator in 30 seconds;
2. watch the complete evidence-aligned story in less than three minutes;
3. distinguish implemented controls, verified evidence, limitations, and future
   direction;
4. find clean-environment setup or judge access instructions;
5. identify Earl Ray as the creator and accountable project owner; and
6. use one functioning, professional contact route.

### Judging-period outcome

Through December 15:

- the exact submitted revision and judge path remain available;
- security and ordinary contact routes are monitored;
- material failures receive a bounded status notice rather than a silent claim
  change;
- interested parties receive a response within two business days when practical;
  and
- conversations and recurring objections are captured privately for product
  learning.

### Commercial-learning targets

These are hypotheses, not competition requirements:

- ten qualified discovery conversations by the end of judging;
- three organizations with a concrete agent-authority or credential problem;
- one bounded design-partner pilot proposal after the competition build is stable;
- documented evidence for or against the initial GitHub/coding-agent wedge; and
- no paid production-security promise before the corresponding product and legal
  readiness exist.

## Stop/go rules

- Stop repository migration if it changes the demo repository identity, GitHub
  App scope, credential enrollment, protected evidence, or exact request without
  a successful rehearsal and explicit approval.
- Stop public claim work when the implementation or claim matrix is unsettled;
  retain placeholders rather than forecasting a pass.
- Stop form or analytics deployment if actual data flow and retention cannot be
  explained accurately.
- Stop accepting a material outside code contribution when submission ownership
  is unclear.
- Stop commercial negotiation before a production-security promise, liability,
  IP transfer, credential handling, or regulated use exceeds the prototype's
  implementation and professional support.
- Stop optional launch polish when it competes with a required development or
  submission gate.

## Definition of complete

This workstream is competition-complete when:

- the visitor journey, quickstart or judge path, video, contact route, and
  submission are functional;
- public claims agree with the exact tagged revision and security-claims matrix;
- the creator, license, provenance, supported platform, limitations, and future
  direction are clear;
- repository, account, domain, and demo mutations have reproducible ownership and
  recovery paths;
- judging-period continuity has an owner and checklist; and
- Earl can respond to serious interest with a discovery guide and bounded pilot
  hypothesis without improvising a production guarantee.

Commercial readiness is not complete merely because the project receives traffic,
stars, a prize, or an introduction. It requires repeated problem evidence and a
buyer willing to fund a bounded outcome.
