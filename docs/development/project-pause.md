# Project pause: the keystone for a future review

Decision date: September 12, 2026. Owner: Earl. Follow-up ownership: [issue #19](https://github.com/Loothore907/guardian-agent/issues/19).

## Decision

Pause Agentic Guardian and its hackathon delivery push indefinitely. There is no
automatic restart date. Prior milestone dates and proposed evaluation campaigns
are historical plans, not current commitments. Do not resume paid experiments,
deployments, resource creation, or scheduled project work without a fresh user
decision and bounded authorization. Shutdown and billing reconciliation remain
active until their outstanding items are resolved.

The user prioritizes eliminating ongoing charges. Preserve source, documentation,
and existing local evidence. Do not maintain paid infrastructure merely to make a
possible restart easier. Identify unique cloud data before proposing irreversible
deletion; distinguish stopped compute from removal of chargeable retained resources.

## Why we paused

More aggressive evaluation assistance was rejected by OpenAI and directed to
Daybreak/Trusted Access enrollment. The user reports enrollment requires two
hardware keys, approximately USD 30 each locally: one daily key and one backup.
The USD 60 expense, application effort, uncertain approval and uncertain research
outcome exceed the commitment the user presently wants to make. The purchase and
application are deferred.

The user is a hackathon builder, not a cybersecurity professional. The broad
authority-layer concept is not established as novel; the distinctiveness and
practical value of this implementation need independent scrutiny. Prior effort
does not create an obligation to continue or spend more.

Restricted access may support a future authorized evaluation, but we have not
established which offering and product surface are required, that approval will
be granted, or that approval would produce a reproducible induction. Do not record
Daybreak as a proven prerequisite for every meaningful Guardian test.

## Evidence we retain, and what it does not establish

- The completed migration ladder fully exposed five attacks to the worker. None
  induced the targeted forbidden proposal: 0/5 induction and no Guardian rejection
  opportunities. See the [migration results](evidence/2026-09-11-t1-migration-escalation-results.md).
- [T0](evidence/2026-09-10-t0-results.md) records the small scripted local
  denial/recovery batch. Scripted proposals do not prove natural injection causation.
- The user's desired demonstration remains a reproducible natural injection that
  induces an actual forbidden typed proposal, followed by Guardian rejection
  before any forbidden effect. The [accepted T1 specification](t1-intervention-evaluation.md)
  additionally requires useful same-session recovery; this pause does not complete
  or silently weaken that gate.
- Broader containment, hosted judge access and general reliability remain subject
  to their documented limitations in [security claims](../security-claims.md).

## Questions to answer before restarting

1. **Distinctive value:** What concrete capability or measured outcome does
   Guardian provide beyond existing agent sandboxes, policy engines and brokers?
   Have an independent reviewer assess both the implementation and alternatives.
2. **Useful audience:** Who needs it, for what actual workflow, and what evidence
   would make them adopt it? Separate a compelling demonstration from product demand.
3. **Decisive experiment:** What smallest authorized experiment would establish
   the missing induction/rejection/recovery chain? Define controls, reproducibility,
   null outcomes and a stop rule before running it.
4. **Access:** Which provider, model and surface permit that experiment? Is
   Daybreak Blue or Red necessary and available for this specific internal work?
   What restrictions apply to any eventual external demo? Do not assume approval
   transfers across models, interfaces or users.
5. **Economics and capacity:** What money and focused time is the user willing to
   risk, including keys, enrollment, compute, review and maintenance? What result
   would justify another increment? No automatic return to the old weekly budget.
6. **Scope and deadline:** Is a hackathon still a useful target? Would one narrow,
   well-evidenced journey be worthwhile if the original ambition remains unproven?
7. **Independent clarity:** Can a security practitioner or fresh reviewer identify
   a fundamental design gap, a better evaluation, or a reason to stop permanently?

top-ups, domain renewal and delayed usage charges separately.

## Restart sequence

Read this document and the billing audit first. Review the evidence and answer the
questions above before buying keys or rebuilding infrastructure. Confirm a new
useful outcome with explicit time, spend, access and integration boundaries.
Then inspect fresh Git/provider state and recreate only the resources that outcome
needs. Treat all old run packets, approvals, deadlines and stopped-resource records
as historical; do not reuse their grants.
